# 01 · Foundations

## What ICT is `[0:02]`
ICT means **following the path smart money leaves behind in price**, with no indicators. Smart money is the
large participants: banks, whales, hedge funds. Most traders lose because they get manipulated out of their
positions. Your edge is learning to read that manipulation so you trade **with** it.

## Liquidity `[0:03] [0:10]`
**Liquidity is the ease at which an asset is bought or sold.** On a chart it is where orders cluster.

| | Buyside liquidity | Sellside liquidity |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Above highs | Below lows |
| Who is there | Shorts' stop-losses (a stop on a short is a **buy**) + breakout buyers | Longs' stop-losses (a stop on a long is a **sell**) + breakdown sellers |
| Smart money uses it to | **Sell** into all that buying | **Buy** from all that selling |

**Think like the whale.** To sell $10 million you need enough buyers to fill you. The only place they gather is
a liquidity pool, so that is where large orders get placed. `[0:04]`

**Price moves to fill orders, not because of patterns.** It travels from one pool to the next, and pools act as
magnets. `[0:05] [0:10]`

### The liquidity cheat sheet `[0:10]`
> **In a bullish market, you buy from sellers, under lows, at sellside liquidity.**
> **In a bearish market, you sell to buyers, above highs, at buyside liquidity.**

### The one question that reads any chart `[0:06] [0:11]`
*How is price reacting to highs and lows?*
- Manipulating highs and displacing lows → **bearish**
- Manipulating lows and displacing highs → **bullish**

Every time price trades above a high is a chance for smart money to sell. Every time it trades below a low is
a chance for smart money to buy. Watch which chance they actually take.

## Candlesticks `[0:07]`
A candle is one window of time on any timeframe. The **body** shows open and close, the **wicks** show the
extremes.

| Expectation | Order of the candle |
|---|---|
| **Bearish** | Open → **High** → Low → Close (jab up first, then drop) |
| **Bullish** | Open → **Low** → High → Close (dip first, then rally) |

This is why the manipulation happens early in a candle: the move against the real direction comes first.

## Swing points `[0:08]`
Keep this simple. It is the definition used everywhere in this guide.
- **Swing high:** a candle with a **lower high on each side**.
- **Swing low:** a candle with a **higher low on each side**.

No extra conditions, and it does not need to make new structure.

## Trapped traders `[0:09]`
Whenever smart money sells, somebody is buying. Those buyers are now trapped, with stops scattered under nearby
lows. When they get stopped out, their forced selling powers the aggressive moves you see on the chart.

Two practical uses:
1. Trapped traders show **where the market is likely to go**: through their stops.
2. They show **where it is unlikely to return**: smart money will not let those buyers out at a profit. They are
   fuel for the move.
